How to Save Tax on FD Interest in India

31 Aug 2026

Fixed Deposits (FDs) are a popular investment choice for many Indians due to their safety and assured returns. However, the interest earned on FDs is fully taxable, which can sometimes reduce the effective returns significantly. Many people are unaware of the rules and strategies that can help them minimize this tax burden. If you're looking for practical ways to keep more of your FD interest income, you're in the right place. This guide will walk you through various legitimate methods to save tax on your FD interest, ensuring your hard-earned money works smarter for you.

Understanding How FD Interest is Taxed

Before we dive into saving tax, it's crucial to understand how FD interest is taxed in India. Interest earned from Fixed Deposits is considered 'Income from Other Sources' and is added to your total annual income. This total income then determines which income tax slab you fall into. For example, if your total income including FD interest pushes you into a higher tax bracket, you'll pay more tax. Banks are required to deduct Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) if your FD interest income from a single bank exceeds Rs. 40,000 in a financial year (Rs. 50,000 for senior citizens). The TDS rate is 10% if you provide your PAN, and 20% if you don't. Even if TDS is deducted, it doesn't mean your tax liability is fully settled; you might still owe more tax if your income falls into a higher slab, or you might be eligible for a refund if too much tax was deducted. This basic understanding is the first step towards effective tax planning.

Utilize Tax-Saving FDs Under Section 80C

One of the most direct ways to save tax using FDs is by investing in a Tax-Saving Fixed Deposit. These FDs come with a mandatory lock-in period of five years. The principal amount invested in these FDs, up to Rs. 1.5 lakh in a financial year, qualifies for a deduction under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act. This means you can reduce your taxable income by the amount invested. It's important to note, however, that while the principal amount invested is eligible for deduction, the interest earned on these FDs is still fully taxable as per your income tax slab. This option is ideal for individuals who are looking for a safe investment option that also helps them utilize their Section 80C limit alongside other popular choices like PPF or ELSS.

Submit Forms 15G and 15H to Avoid TDS

If your total annual income is below the basic exemption limit (which means you have no tax liability), you can prevent banks from deducting TDS on your FD interest. This is done by submitting Form 15G (for general citizens) or Form 15H (for senior citizens). You must declare in these forms that your total income for the financial year will be below the taxable threshold. It's crucial to submit these forms to each bank where you hold FDs at the beginning of every financial year. If you fail to submit these forms and TDS is deducted, you would then have to claim a refund by filing your Income Tax Return. Submitting these forms timely helps you avoid the hassle of claiming refunds and ensures you receive your full interest amount.

Strategize with Multiple FDs and Family Accounts

A smart strategy to manage TDS and tax on FD interest is to diversify your FD investments. Instead of putting a large sum in one FD with a single bank, consider breaking it into multiple smaller FDs across different banks or even within the same bank with different maturity dates. This helps keep the interest income from each individual FD or bank below the TDS threshold of Rs. 40,000 (or Rs. 50,000 for seniors). Additionally, if you have a spouse who is in a lower income tax bracket or has no taxable income, you can consider investing FDs in their name. However, be aware of income clubbing rules: if you transfer money to your spouse for investment, the income generated is generally clubbed back with your income. This rule doesn't apply if the funds are genuinely from your spouse's independent income or if you gift them the amount and they invest it themselves without any strings attached.

Choose Cumulative FDs for Deferred Tax Liability

FDs can generally be categorised into two types based on interest payout: cumulative and non-cumulative. In a non-cumulative FD, interest is paid out periodically (monthly, quarterly, half-yearly, or annually) and is taxable in the year it is received. In contrast, a cumulative FD allows the interest to compound and be reinvested with the principal until maturity. The entire accumulated interest is then paid out at the end of the term. While the interest is still taxable, opting for a cumulative FD defers your tax liability until the year of maturity. This strategy doesn't reduce your overall tax, but it gives you the benefit of compounding on the full interest amount for a longer period and postpones the tax payment, which can be advantageous for tax planning.

Review Your Overall Taxable Income Slab

Since FD interest is added to your total income and taxed as per your applicable slab rate, managing your overall taxable income indirectly helps in saving tax on FD interest. By effectively utilizing other deductions and exemptions available under various sections of the Income Tax Act, such as Section 80C (for investments like PPF, ELSS, life insurance premiums), Section 80D (for health insurance premiums), HRA exemption, LTA exemption, etc., you can bring down your overall taxable income. If your total taxable income falls into a lower slab, the effective tax rate applied to your FD interest will also be lower. Therefore, a holistic approach to tax planning, considering all your income sources and available deductions, is essential for optimizing your tax outgo on FD interest and other income streams.

Conclusion

Saving tax on your FD interest requires understanding the rules and applying smart financial planning. From utilizing specific tax-saving FDs under Section 80C to strategically submitting Forms 15G/15H and carefully choosing cumulative options, there are several legitimate avenues to minimize your tax burden. Remember that while these strategies can help, they should always be part of a broader financial plan. Always keep yourself informed about the latest tax regulations and consider your personal financial situation to make the most informed decisions for your investments.
