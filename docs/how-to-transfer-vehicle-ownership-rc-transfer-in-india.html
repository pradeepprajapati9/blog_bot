How to Transfer Vehicle Ownership (RC Transfer) in India

30 Oct 2026

Transferring vehicle ownership, commonly known as RC transfer, is a mandatory and crucial step when buying or selling a used vehicle in India. Neglecting this process can lead to significant legal and financial complications for both the buyer and the seller. Whether you've just bought your dream pre-owned car or sold your old bike, ensuring the vehicle's Registration Certificate (RC) is updated with the new owner's name is paramount. This guide will walk you through the entire process, making it simple to understand and follow.

Why RC Transfer is Essential for Both Parties

The Registration Certificate (RC) is the legal document that proves ownership of a vehicle. Failing to transfer this ownership can expose both the buyer and seller to various risks. For the seller, if the vehicle is involved in an accident or illegal activity after the sale, but before the RC transfer, they could still be held liable. This includes traffic violations, criminal acts, or even unpaid tolls. For the buyer, not having the RC in their name means they don't legally own the vehicle. This can complicate insurance claims, resale, and even basic services like getting new number plates or a Pollution Under Control (PUC) certificate. Completing the RC transfer ensures legal compliance, avoids future disputes, and protects both parties from unforeseen liabilities and administrative headaches.

Key Documents Required from the Seller

As a seller, you play a crucial role in initiating the RC transfer. Ensure you have the following documents ready: Original Registration Certificate (RC), ensuring it's not a duplicate. Form 29 (Notice of Transfer of Ownership) and Form 30 (Application for Intimation and Transfer of Ownership) – both in duplicate. A valid Pollution Under Control (PUC) certificate. Valid insurance policy copy. Your PAN card copy or Form 60/61. Your Aadhaar card copy. Two passport-sized photographs. An NOC (No Objection Certificate) from the financier if the vehicle was bought on loan (Form 35 and bank letter stating loan closure). An affidavit declaring the sale and vehicle condition might also be required in some states. Ensure all forms are accurately filled and signed.

Key Documents Required from the Buyer

As the buyer, you will also need to furnish several documents to complete the RC transfer process. These typically include: Form 29 (in duplicate) and Form 30 (in duplicate), duly signed by both you and the seller. Your address proof (Aadhaar card, utility bill, rent agreement). Your identity proof (PAN card, Aadhaar card, driving license). Two passport-sized photographs. The valid insurance policy copy. The valid PUC certificate of the vehicle. A blank envelope with postage stamps and your address for receiving the new RC. If the vehicle is registered in another state, an NOC from the original RTO might be necessary, and you would need to re-register it in your state within a specific timeframe. Always verify state-specific requirements.

The Online Application Process via Parivahan Portal

The first step for RC transfer often begins online. Visit the official Parivahan Sewa website (parivahan.gov.in). Navigate to 'Online Services' and then select 'Vehicle Related Services'. You'll need to enter the vehicle's registration number and proceed. Look for the 'Transfer of Ownership' option. Fill in the required details, including buyer and seller information, vehicle details, and current address. You will need to upload scanned copies of all necessary documents. Carefully review all entries before submitting the application. Once submitted, you will typically receive an application ID and have the option to pay the transfer fees online. Keep a printout of the online application form and the payment receipt; these will be required for the physical submission at the RTO.

Physical RTO Submission and Verification

After completing the online application and payment, the next crucial step is the physical submission of documents at your local Regional Transport Office (RTO). Both the buyer and seller (or their authorized representatives) might need to be present, especially during the initial stages. Bundle all your original documents along with their photocopies, including the printout of the online application and fee receipt. Submit these to the designated counter at the RTO. An RTO official will verify all documents and signatures. In some cases, a vehicle inspection might be required, especially if the vehicle is old or from a different state. If all documents are in order and verified, the RTO will process your application. You might receive a receipt with a tentative date for the new RC issuance or a tracking number.

Fees, Timelines, and Important Considerations

The fees for RC transfer vary based on the vehicle type (two-wheeler, four-wheeler) and the state. Typically, it includes a transfer fee and smart card fee. Road tax might also be applicable if the vehicle is being transferred from another state. You can usually find the exact fee structure on the Parivahan portal or your state's transport department website. The processing time for RC transfer can range from 15 days to 30 days, sometimes longer, depending on the RTO's workload and verification process. During this period, you can often track the status of your application online. It is crucial to complete the transfer within 14 days of the sale date to avoid penalties. Also, remember to get your vehicle's insurance updated with the new owner's details once the RC transfer is complete.

What if There's a Loan (Hypothecation) on the Vehicle?

If the vehicle was originally purchased on a loan, its Registration Certificate (RC) will have an endorsement of 'Hypothecation' in favor of the financing bank. Before the RC can be transferred to a new owner, this hypothecation must be removed. The seller needs to obtain a No Objection Certificate (NOC) from their bank, confirming that the loan has been fully repaid. This NOC, along with Form 35 (Application for Cancellation of Hypothecation), must be submitted to the RTO to get the hypothecation removed from the RC. Only after the hypothecation is officially cancelled can the RC transfer process to the new buyer be initiated. This step is critical and often overlooked, leading to delays.

Conclusion

Successfully transferring vehicle ownership is a responsibility for both the buyer and the seller. By following these steps and ensuring all documents are correctly filled and submitted, you can complete the RC transfer process smoothly and avoid any legal hassles. Always verify state-specific requirements and timelines with your local RTO or the Parivahan Sewa website for the most accurate and up-to-date information. A proper RC transfer ensures peace of mind and full legal ownership for the new vehicle owner.
