How to Save Tax on Capital Gains in India

12 Sep 2026

When you sell an asset like a house, shares, mutual funds, or even gold, for a profit, this profit is called a 'capital gain'. While it's great to make a profit, these gains are generally taxable in India. Understanding how to legally reduce this tax burden is a smart financial move that can significantly increase your net earnings. This guide will walk you through practical, accurate methods available to Indian taxpayers to save tax on capital gains, helping you keep more of your hard-earned money.

Understanding Capital Gains: Short-term vs. Long-term

To save tax effectively, first understand the two types of capital gains: Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) and Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG). The distinction depends on how long you held the asset before selling it. For listed shares and equity-oriented mutual funds, a holding period of up to 12 months results in STCG, while more than 12 months results in LTCG. For real estate, debt mutual funds, and other assets like gold, the threshold is typically 24 or 36 months, depending on the asset. STCG is usually taxed at your income tax slab rate, or a special rate like 15% for equity. LTCG on equity over ₹1 Lakh in a financial year is taxed at 10% without indexation, while for other assets, LTCG is usually taxed at 20% after applying indexation benefit. Knowing this distinction is crucial as the tax rules and saving options differ for each.

Section 54 Exemption: Reinvesting in a Residential House

One of the most popular ways to save tax on Long-Term Capital Gains arising from the sale of a residential house is through Section 54 of the Income Tax Act. If you sell your residential property and make a capital gain, you can claim an exemption if you reinvest the entire capital gain amount into buying or constructing another residential house in India. The new house must be purchased either one year before or two years after the date of sale, or constructed within three years from the date of sale. If you haven't invested the entire amount before filing your Income Tax Return, you must deposit it into a Capital Gains Account Scheme with a public sector bank. This ensures the funds are set aside for the specified purpose, and you can still claim the exemption.

Section 54F Exemption: Selling Other Assets to Buy a House

What if you sell a long-term capital asset other than a residential house, such as a plot of land, commercial property, shares, mutual funds (non-equity), or even gold, and want to save tax? Section 54F comes to your rescue. Under this section, if you use the net sale consideration (not just the capital gain) from selling any long-term asset to purchase or construct a new residential house in India, you can claim an exemption. The conditions for timelines (one year before or two years after sale for purchase, three years for construction) and the Capital Gains Account Scheme are similar to Section 54. The exemption amount is proportionate to the amount of net sale consideration reinvested. This section is particularly useful for diversifying your asset portfolio while saving tax.

Section 54EC Bonds: Strategic Reinvestment Options

For those who prefer a more conservative reinvestment option for their Long-Term Capital Gains from any asset (property, shares, gold, etc.), Section 54EC offers an alternative. You can invest the capital gain amount into specific government-notified bonds, such as those issued by the National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) or Rural Electrification Corporation (REC). The investment must be made within six months from the date of sale of the original asset. There's a maximum investment limit of ₹50 lakhs in these bonds per financial year. While these bonds typically offer a lower rate of interest compared to other investments, they come with a 5-year lock-in period and are completely exempt from capital gains tax, making them a safe and effective tax-saving instrument.

Utilizing Capital Losses: Set-off and Carry Forward

Not all investments result in gains; sometimes, you incur a capital loss. The good news is that you can use these losses to reduce your tax liability on capital gains. Short-Term Capital Losses (STCL) can be set off against both Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) and Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG). However, Long-Term Capital Losses (LTCL) can only be set off against Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG). If you cannot fully adjust your capital losses in the current financial year, you can carry them forward for up to eight subsequent assessment years. To avail this benefit, it is crucial to file your Income Tax Return (ITR) on time, even if your total income is below the taxable limit, ensuring your losses are properly declared.

The Power of Indexation: Reducing Your Taxable Gain

Indexation is a powerful tool to reduce your Long-Term Capital Gains tax liability, especially for assets like real estate (other than residential house sold for Section 54), debt mutual funds, and gold. It allows you to adjust the cost of acquisition of your asset for inflation over the holding period, using the Cost Inflation Index (CII) provided by the income tax department. By inflating your purchase price to reflect its value in current terms, your 'indexed cost of acquisition' increases, thereby reducing the actual taxable capital gain. For example, if you bought land years ago, indexation significantly lowers the gain you declare, leading to lower tax. This benefit is usually applicable when LTCG is taxed at 20% and not for listed equity shares or equity-oriented mutual funds which follow a different taxation rule.

Conclusion

Saving tax on capital gains doesn't have to be complicated. By understanding the type of gain you've made and the various exemptions and provisions available under the Indian Income Tax Act, you can make informed decisions to legally reduce your tax burden. Whether it's reinvesting in a new home, opting for specific bonds, or strategically managing your capital losses, these methods empower you to retain more of your investment profits. Always ensure you maintain proper records of your transactions and consult reliable sources for the latest tax rules. Smart planning today can lead to significant savings tomorrow.
