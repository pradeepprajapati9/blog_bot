How to File RTI Online in India: A Simple Guide

22 Sep 2026

The Right to Information (RTI) Act, 2005, is a powerful tool designed to promote transparency and accountability in governance. As an Indian citizen, it empowers you to request information from public authorities, shedding light on their functions and decisions. While the concept might seem complex, filing an RTI application has become significantly easier, especially with the introduction of the online portal. This guide will walk you through the entire process of filing an RTI online, ensuring you can confidently exercise your right to access crucial public information.

What is the Right to Information (RTI) Act?

The Right to Information Act, enacted in 2005, grants Indian citizens the statutory right to access information held by any public authority. Its primary objective is to foster transparency and accountability in the working of every public authority, combating corruption and promoting good governance. This means you can request government records, documents, notes, circulars, reports, and other material information that concerns public interest. Any Indian citizen can file an RTI application. The Act mandates timely responses from government departments and agencies, ensuring that information is not withheld arbitrarily. Understanding the core purpose of RTI is the first step towards effectively using this vital democratic tool to seek clarity and hold authorities responsible.

Information You Can (and Cannot) Request

When filing an RTI, it's crucial to understand the scope of information you can seek. Generally, you can request any information that is held by a 'public authority' – this includes central, state, and local governments, government-owned companies, and non-governmental organizations substantially financed by the government. The information must already exist in a documented form; you cannot ask for opinions, explanations, or analyses. However, there are specific exemptions under Sections 8 and 9 of the RTI Act. You cannot request information that pertains to national security, integrity of the country, commercial confidence, trade secrets, intellectual property, or personal information that has no relationship to any public activity or interest. Being specific and clear about your request within these boundaries will lead to a more effective RTI application.

Essential Details for Your Online RTI Application

To file your RTI online, you'll need a few key pieces of information ready. Firstly, your personal details such as your full name, complete address, email ID, and mobile number are mandatory for correspondence. Secondly, you must clearly identify the specific Central Ministry, Department, or Public Authority from which you are seeking information. The online portal offers a dropdown list to help you select the correct one. Most importantly, you need to formulate your question(s) clearly, concisely, and precisely in the "Text of Application" box. Avoid ambiguity or overly broad queries, as this can lead to delays or rejection. While no "documents" in the traditional sense are needed to initiate the application, having your questions well-defined beforehand is crucial for a smooth process.

Step-by-Step Guide to Filing RTI Online

Filing an RTI online is a straightforward process through the official rtionline.gov.in portal. 1. **Visit the Portal:** Open your web browser and navigate to rtionline.gov.in. 2. **Click 'Submit Request':** On the homepage, click on the "Submit Request" option. 3. **Read Guidelines:** Carefully read the guidelines for filing RTI requests and tick the checkbox stating you have read and understood them. Then click "Submit." 4. **Select Public Authority:** From the dropdown menu, choose the appropriate Ministry/Department/Public Authority. If you're unsure, try to narrow it down based on the subject of your query. 5. **Fill Application Form:** Enter your personal details (name, address, email, phone number) accurately. In the "Text of Application" box, type your specific question(s). Keep it concise. 6. **Supporting Document (Optional):** If you have any supporting documents (e.g., related to a previous complaint), you can upload them here, but it's often not required. 7. **Security Code & Payment:** Enter the security code displayed, then click "Make Payment." Follow the instructions to pay the application fee, typically ₹10. 8. **Submit & Note Registration Number:** After successful payment, you will receive a unique registration number. Make sure to save this number carefully, as it's essential for tracking your application status.

Understanding RTI Application Fees and Payment

A nominal fee is typically associated with filing an RTI application in India, set at ₹10 for Central Government departments. This fee can be paid conveniently online through various methods available on the rtionline.gov.in portal. You can use Net Banking from major banks, credit cards (Visa, MasterCard, RuPay), or debit cards (Maestro, RuPay). It's important to complete the payment within the stipulated time frame once you initiate the transaction to avoid your application session expiring. Citizens holding a Below Poverty Line (BPL) card are exempted from paying this application fee. If you fall into this category, you will need to indicate this in the form and provide your BPL card number. After the initial application, if the Public Information Officer (PIO) needs to provide copies of large documents, additional fees may be requested for reproduction.

Tracking Your RTI Application Status Online

Once you've successfully filed your RTI application and received your unique registration number, the next step is to track its progress. The official rtionline.gov.in portal offers a dedicated "View Status" option. Simply enter your registration number and the security code, and you can instantly check the current status of your request. According to the RTI Act, the Public Information Officer (PIO) is mandated to respond to your application within 30 days of receipt. If the information concerns the life or liberty of a person, the response period is reduced to 48 hours. If you do not receive a response within the stipulated time, or if you are dissatisfied with the response provided, you have the right to file a First Appeal with the First Appellate Authority within the same public authority. This ensures accountability and provides a mechanism for recourse.

Conclusion

The Right to Information Act is a cornerstone of India's democracy, empowering every citizen to hold public authorities accountable and ensure transparency. By following this simple online guide, you can confidently file your RTI application and seek the information you need. Remember to be clear and specific in your questions, understand the permissible scope of information, and keep track of your application. Exercising your right to information not only helps you gain insights but also contributes to a more open, responsible, and democratic governance system in India.
