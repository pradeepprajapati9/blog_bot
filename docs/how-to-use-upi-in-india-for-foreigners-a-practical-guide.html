How to Use UPI in India for Foreigners: A Practical Guide

02 Aug 2026

UPI (Unified Payments Interface) has transformed digital payments in India, making transactions incredibly fast, secure, and convenient. From street vendors to large stores, UPI is everywhere. Naturally, foreign visitors and non-resident Indians (NRIs) often wonder if they can also tap into this seamless payment system during their stay. While UPI offers immense convenience, its accessibility for foreigners comes with specific requirements and some limitations. This guide breaks down exactly how UPI works for different types of foreign nationals in India.

Understanding the Core Requirement: Indian Bank Account & Mobile Number

At its heart, UPI is designed to link directly to an Indian bank account. To use any UPI application, you must first have a valid bank account with an Indian bank. Furthermore, this bank account needs to be linked to an active Indian mobile number. This mobile number acts as your primary identifier for UPI, and it's essential for receiving OTPs (One-Time Passwords) for registration and authentication. Without these two fundamental elements – an Indian bank account and an Indian mobile number registered with that account – you generally cannot set up and use UPI for transactions in India. This is the primary hurdle for most foreign tourists visiting the country for a short duration.

UPI for Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) and PIOs

If you are a Non-Resident Indian (NRI) or a Person of Indian Origin (PIO) and maintain an NRE (Non-Resident External) or NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) bank account in India, you are generally eligible to use UPI. The process is straightforward: first, ensure your NRE/NRO account is linked to an active Indian mobile number. Many banks now allow international mobile numbers from certain countries to be linked to NRE/NRO accounts for UPI. Once that's set, download any popular UPI app like Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm, or BHIM. Register using your Indian mobile number, select your bank, and set up your UPI PIN. You can then make payments directly from your Indian bank account, enjoying the full convenience of UPI just like a resident Indian. This makes managing finances in India much easier for NRIs and PIOs.

The Challenge for Short-Term Foreign Tourists

For foreign tourists on a short visit to India who do not possess an Indian bank account or an Indian mobile number linked to one, using standard UPI apps like Google Pay or PhonePe is currently not possible. The system is fundamentally built around India-specific financial infrastructure. Tourists cannot simply link their international bank accounts or foreign mobile numbers to existing UPI platforms to make payments. This means that while UPI is ubiquitous for locals, it poses a significant barrier for most international visitors. It's crucial for tourists to understand this limitation to avoid frustration and plan their payment methods accordingly during their trip to India.

Emerging Solutions: Limited Options for Foreign Visitors

While direct UPI access for foreign tourists remains limited, the Indian government and banking regulators are exploring solutions. The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and NPCI (National Payments Corporation of India) have initiated pilot programs to allow inbound foreign travellers from G20 countries to link their international debit/credit cards to UPI-enabled apps for merchant payments. These initiatives aim to provide a more seamless experience. However, these are often in a pilot phase, may require specific apps or partnerships, and are not yet universally available or easily set up by every tourist upon arrival. It’s advisable to check the latest official announcements or with your bank before traveling, but do not rely solely on this option unless you have made specific arrangements.

Practical Payment Alternatives for Foreigners in India

Given the current limitations of UPI for most foreign tourists, it's wise to plan for alternative payment methods. The most universally accepted method is cash. Always carry a sufficient amount of Indian Rupees for smaller transactions, street vendors, local transport, and places that might not accept cards. International credit and debit cards (Visa, MasterCard, American Express) are widely accepted at larger stores, hotels, restaurants, and online platforms. You can also withdraw cash from ATMs using your international debit card. It's recommended to inform your bank of your travel plans to avoid card blocks due to suspicious activity. Having a mix of cash and international cards ensures you're prepared for any payment scenario.

Staying Updated and Planning Your Finances

The landscape of digital payments is constantly evolving, and India is at the forefront of this change. While the immediate ease of UPI access for all foreigners is still developing, the intent to broaden its reach is clear. For now, the most practical approach for foreign visitors is to understand the current requirements and limitations. Plan your finances by relying on cash and international credit/debit cards as your primary payment methods. For NRIs and PIOs with Indian bank accounts, UPI remains a highly convenient and accessible tool. Always refer to official government and banking websites for the most current and accurate information regarding UPI usage for non-residents.

Conclusion

UPI is undoubtedly a game-changer in India's digital economy, offering unparalleled convenience for those with an Indian banking presence. For Non-Resident Indians and Persons of Indian Origin with an NRE/NRO account and an Indian mobile number, UPI is readily accessible and highly recommended. However, for most foreign tourists without an Indian bank account, using UPI directly remains a challenge, with pilot programs slowly paving the way for future integration. Until these solutions are widely implemented, carrying cash and using international credit/debit cards are your most reliable payment options for a smooth and enjoyable trip across India.
