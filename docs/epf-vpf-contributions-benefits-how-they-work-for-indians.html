EPF & VPF: Contributions, Benefits & How They Work for Indians

27 Sep 2026

For many salaried individuals in India, the Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) is a cornerstone of their retirement savings, often deducted automatically from their monthly salary. But beyond the mandatory deduction, how well do you truly understand EPF and its voluntary counterpart, the Voluntary Provident Fund (VPF)? These funds play a crucial role in building long-term financial security, offering attractive interest rates and tax benefits. This guide will break down the essentials of EPF and VPF, explaining their contributions, benefits, and how you can leverage them for a more secure financial future. It's time to understand where your money goes and how it grows for you.

What is EPF? Your Retirement Foundation

The Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) is a mandatory savings scheme for salaried employees in India, managed by the Employees' Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO). Its primary goal is to provide social security and a lump sum amount upon retirement. If you work in an establishment with 20 or more employees, and your basic salary plus dearness allowance is up to ₹15,000 per month, EPF contribution is compulsory. Even if your salary exceeds this threshold, you can still contribute to EPF. Both the employee and the employer contribute to your EPF account, ensuring a steady accumulation of funds over your working life. This scheme acts as a powerful, government-backed tool to help you save for the long term, offering a disciplined approach to building your retirement corpus.

Decoding Your EPF Contributions

Understanding the split of your EPF contribution is key. Currently, both the employee and the employer contribute 12% of the employee's basic salary plus dearness allowance to the provident fund. However, the employer's 12% contribution is further divided: 8.33% goes towards the Employees' Pension Scheme (EPS), which provides a pension upon retirement, and the remaining 3.67% goes into your EPF account. The employee's entire 12% contribution goes directly into the EPF account. This structure ensures you benefit from both a provident fund for a lump sum payout and a pension scheme for regular income post-retirement. These contributions are directly deducted from your salary each month, growing steadily over time.

Introducing Voluntary Provident Fund (VPF)

While EPF is mandatory up to a certain limit, the Voluntary Provident Fund (VPF) offers you the flexibility to increase your savings further. VPF is essentially an extension of your EPF account, allowing you to contribute more than the mandatory 12% towards your provident fund. It is an optional scheme, open only to salaried employees who are already contributing to EPF. The key difference is that while EPF has a fixed contribution rate for both employer and employee, VPF contributions come solely from the employee. You can contribute up to 100% of your basic salary plus dearness allowance to VPF, providing a significant avenue for disciplined, tax-efficient savings.

How to Contribute to VPF

Contributing to VPF is a straightforward process that you initiate through your employer. Unlike EPF, where contributions are automatic, you need to inform your employer about your decision to opt for VPF. Typically, you would submit a request or fill out a form provided by your company's HR or payroll department, specifying the percentage of your basic salary plus dearness allowance you wish to contribute as VPF. This percentage can range from 1% up to 100%. Once you've made this declaration, the additional VPF amount will be deducted directly from your salary along with your regular EPF contribution. Most companies allow employees to start or stop VPF contributions once a year, usually at the beginning of the financial year. It’s a flexible way to boost your retirement savings.

Key Benefits of EPF & VPF

Both EPF and VPF offer a compelling set of benefits that make them attractive savings instruments. Firstly, they provide **tax benefits** under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act, allowing you to deduct contributions up to ₹1.5 lakh from your taxable income annually. Secondly, the **interest earned** on both EPF and VPF is generally tax-exempt, provided certain conditions are met regarding withdrawal after 5 years of service. The government declares the interest rate for EPF annually, which has historically been competitive and higher than many other fixed-income options. Thirdly, these funds promote **long-term wealth creation** through compounding, as interest is earned not just on your principal but also on accumulated interest. Lastly, they offer **financial security** as a safe, government-backed savings avenue for your retirement.

EPF vs. VPF: Making the Right Choice for You

While both EPF and VPF contribute to your provident fund and share similar benefits like tax savings and attractive interest rates, understanding their nuances helps you make informed choices. EPF is mandatory for eligible employees, with fixed contributions from both you and your employer. It's your baseline for retirement savings. VPF, on the other hand, is purely voluntary and allows you, the employee, to contribute *additional* funds beyond the mandatory EPF. If you have surplus income after meeting your monthly expenses and other financial goals, and you're looking for a low-risk, tax-efficient way to save more for retirement, VPF can be an excellent option. It offers the same benefits as EPF, but with greater flexibility in contribution amount, making it a powerful tool for accelerating your retirement corpus.

Checking Your EPF & VPF Balance

Keeping track of your EPF and VPF balance is simple and transparent. The EPFO provides several easy ways for members to check their accumulated balance. The most common method is through the **UMANG App**, a single platform for various government services. You can also visit the **EPFO Member e-Sewa portal** online, where after activating your Universal Account Number (UAN), you can view your passbook. Additionally, you can receive your balance details via an **SMS** by sending 'EPFOHO UAN ENG' to 7738299899 from your registered mobile number. For those who prefer a call, a **missed call service** is also available at 011-22901406 from your registered mobile number. These methods ensure you always have access to your provident fund details and can monitor the growth of your retirement savings.

Conclusion

EPF and VPF are more than just deductions from your salary; they are powerful instruments designed to secure your financial future. By understanding how your contributions work, the attractive benefits they offer, and how VPF can be leveraged for additional savings, you gain greater control over your retirement planning. Regularly checking your balance and making informed decisions about VPF contributions can significantly boost your long-term wealth. Embrace these provident fund options as a core part of your financial strategy to build a robust and secure tomorrow for yourself and your family.
