How to Check Form 26AS Online in India: Your Guide

23 Oct 2026

Understanding your tax situation is a vital part of personal finance. Form 26AS is an annual consolidated statement that provides details of tax deducted or collected at source on your PAN, along with other high-value financial transactions. For every Indian taxpayer, accessing and reviewing this document is absolutely crucial before filing your Income Tax Return (ITR). It helps you reconcile your tax credits and ensures that your tax filings are accurate and complete. This guide will walk you through the simple, step-by-step process of checking your Form 26AS online, empowering you to manage your taxes effectively.

What is Form 26AS and Why is it Important?

Form 26AS is your Annual Consolidated Tax Statement, available for every Permanent Account Number (PAN) holder. It’s like a passbook for all tax-related transactions made against your PAN. This statement shows details of Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) by your employer, banks, or other entities, and Tax Collected at Source (TCS) by sellers. It also includes details of any Advance Tax or Self-Assessment Tax you have paid, and even information about high-value transactions like property purchases or sale of shares. Its importance cannot be overstated: it acts as a verification document. By checking your Form 26AS, you can ensure that all taxes deducted from your income have been correctly reported to the Income Tax Department. This reconciliation prevents discrepancies, helps you claim the correct tax credits, and ensures a smooth tax filing process, avoiding potential notices from the tax authorities.

Prerequisites for Accessing Your Form 26AS Online

Before you begin the process of checking your Form 26AS, ensure you have a few essential things ready. Firstly, your Permanent Account Number (PAN) card is absolutely mandatory, as Form 26AS is linked directly to your PAN. Secondly, you need to have a registered account on the official Income Tax e-filing portal of India. If you haven't registered yet, you will need to do so first using your PAN. Alternatively, you can also access Form 26AS through your bank's Net Banking facility, provided your PAN is linked to your bank account and your bank offers this service. Most major banks do. Lastly, a stable internet connection and a device like a computer or smartphone are necessary to navigate the online portals. Having these prerequisites in place will make the process quick and hassle-free.

Method 1: Accessing Form 26AS via Income Tax e-filing Portal

The primary and most reliable way to access your Form 26AS is through the official Income Tax e-filing portal. Begin by visiting the website at incometax.gov.in. Log in to your account using your PAN as your User ID and your password. If you're a new user, you'll need to register first. Once logged in, navigate to the 'e-File' menu. From the dropdown, select 'Income Tax Returns' and then 'View Form 26AS'. You will be redirected to the TRACES portal (TDS Reconciliation Analysis and Correction Enabling System). On the TRACES portal, confirm the disclaimer and click 'Proceed'. Choose the 'Assessment Year' for which you want to view the statement (e.g., for income earned in FY 2023-24, select AY 2024-25). Then, select the 'View Type' as HTML to see it online or PDF to download. After selecting, click 'View/Download' to see or save your Form 26AS.

Method 2: Through Your Net Banking Account

For many taxpayers, accessing Form 26AS through their Net Banking account is a convenient alternative. This method is available if your PAN is linked to your bank account, and your bank is registered with the Income Tax Department to provide this facility. Most major public and private sector banks offer this service. To begin, log in to your internet banking account using your credentials. Once logged in, look for a section related to 'Tax Credit Statement' or 'View 26AS' or 'Tax Services'. The exact option name might vary slightly depending on your bank's interface. Once you locate it, click on it, and you will typically be redirected to the TRACES website from your bank's secure portal. You'll then follow similar steps as Method 1 to select the Assessment Year and view or download your Form 26AS. This method simplifies access by leveraging your existing bank login, eliminating the need to remember a separate password for the income tax portal.

Understanding Your Form 26AS Report

Once you have opened or downloaded your Form 26AS, it’s important to understand its different sections. The report is typically divided into several parts. Part A details TDS on salary, interest income, pension, etc., showing the deductor's name, TAN, the amount of tax deducted, and deposited. Part B covers TCS, if applicable. Part C shows details of any tax paid by you directly, such as Advance Tax or Self-Assessment Tax. Part D includes details of paid refund, if any. Part E contains information on high-value financial transactions. When reviewing, always check your PAN number, the Assessment Year, and ensure that all tax deductions and payments match your records, especially your Form 16 from your employer or Form 16A from other deductees like banks. This comprehensive check helps in accurate ITR filing.

What to Do if There are Discrepancies in Form 26AS

Finding discrepancies in your Form 26AS can be concerning, but it's important to know how to address them. If the tax credit shown in your Form 26AS is less than what is mentioned in your Form 16/16A, or if there are errors in the details of the tax deducted, you must take action. First, immediately contact the deductor (your employer, bank, or other entity that deducted tax) and request them to correct the error by filing a revised TDS return. Once they make the correction, it typically reflects in your Form 26AS within a few days. Do not file your Income Tax Return until the discrepancies are resolved, as filing with mismatched information can lead to processing delays or notices from the Income Tax Department. If the deductor is unresponsive, you can also raise a grievance on the Income Tax e-filing portal under the 'Grievances' section, providing all relevant details.

Key Takeaways for Regular Monitoring

Regularly checking your Form 26AS is a smart financial habit that empowers you as a taxpayer. It's advisable to view your Form 26AS at least twice a year: once mid-year to ensure your TDS is being correctly deposited, and again just before filing your Income Tax Return. This proactive approach helps you catch any errors early and provides ample time for correction. Remember, your Form 26AS is the authoritative statement for tax credits in the eyes of the Income Tax Department. Ensuring its accuracy is paramount for a smooth and compliant tax filing experience. By following the simple steps outlined, you can take control of your tax information and avoid unnecessary stress during the tax season. Make it a routine to check this document, and you'll simplify your financial life significantly.

Conclusion

Form 26AS is an indispensable tool for every Indian taxpayer, offering a transparent view of your tax credits and financial transactions. By understanding its contents and knowing how to access it through either the Income Tax e-filing portal or your Net Banking account, you equip yourself with vital knowledge for accurate tax planning and filing. We hope this guide has demystified the process, enabling you to confidently monitor your tax position and ensure a hassle-free interaction with the tax authorities. Stay informed, stay compliant, and manage your finances with greater ease.
