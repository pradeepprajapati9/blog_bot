How to Save Tax on Salary in India

18 Sep 2026

Paying income tax is a reality for most salaried individuals in India. While taxes are a contribution to the nation, understanding how to legitimately reduce your tax burden can significantly improve your take-home salary. This guide will walk you through common and effective methods for saving tax on your salary, focusing on legally permissible deductions and exemptions available under the Income Tax Act. We aim to provide you with accurate and practical information to help you make informed decisions throughout the financial year.

Understanding Income Tax Deductions

The Indian Income Tax Act allows individuals to reduce their taxable income through various deductions. These deductions effectively lower the amount of your salary on which tax is calculated. The most common deductions are available under Chapter VI-A of the Income Tax Act. It's crucial to keep proper documentation for all expenses and investments you plan to claim as deductions. Your employer usually asks for these proofs during a specific period in the financial year, often towards the end. Understanding which deductions apply to you and planning them in advance can make a significant difference in your tax outflow. Familiarise yourself with the sections that offer these benefits so you can maximize your savings.

Section 80C: Your Tax-Saving Powerhouse

Section 80C is perhaps the most popular section for tax savings. It allows you to claim deductions up to a maximum of ₹1.5 lakh per financial year. This limit is for the aggregate of investments and expenses made under various sub-sections. Popular options include investments in Public Provident Fund (PPF), Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS) mutual funds, National Savings Certificates (NSC), life insurance premiums, and tuition fees for children. Repayment of principal on home loans also qualifies. Planning your investments in instruments covered under Section 80C early in the financial year will ensure you utilize the full benefit without last-minute rushes.

NPS and Section 80CCD

The National Pension System (NPS) offers additional tax benefits beyond Section 80C. Under Section 80CCD(1), you can claim a deduction for your contribution to NPS, up to 10% of your salary (basic + dearness allowance). Further, Section 80CCD(1B) provides an additional deduction of up to ₹50,000 for contributions to NPS. This means you can save tax on an amount exceeding the ₹1.5 lakh limit of Section 80C. NPS is a long-term investment designed for retirement, so consider its suitability for your financial goals while availing these tax benefits.

House Rent Allowance (HRA) Exemption

If you receive House Rent Allowance (HRA) as part of your salary and live in a rented accommodation, you can claim an exemption on the HRA received. This exemption is calculated based on the least of three factors: the actual HRA received, 50% of salary (if living in a metro city) or 40% of salary (if living in a non-metro city), and the actual rent paid minus 10% of salary. To claim this exemption, you must submit rent receipts and the landlord's PAN card details if the annual rent exceeds ₹1 lakh. This is a significant way to reduce your taxable salary if you are paying rent.

Standard Deduction and Other Allowances

Every salaried individual is eligible for a Standard Deduction of ₹50,000. This deduction is automatically available and reduces your gross salary before calculating your taxable income. Apart from HRA, other allowances like Leave Travel Allowance (LTA) can also offer tax benefits, subject to certain conditions and limits, provided they are part of your salary structure. LTA can be claimed twice in a block of four calendar years for travel within India. While these might not require specific investments, understanding their eligibility and documentation requirements is important for maximizing tax savings.

Tax Saving Investments for Employees

Besides the common deductions, explore specific tax-saving investment options that might be available through your employer or align with your financial planning. For instance, if your company offers a health insurance plan, the premium paid by the employer is usually tax-exempt for the employee. Similarly, some companies offer benefits like fuel reimbursements or allowances for specific job-related expenses, which can also be structured to be tax-efficient. Always verify the tax treatment of any allowance or benefit with your HR department or a tax professional to ensure compliance and optimal savings.

Conclusion

Saving tax on your salary is achievable with careful planning and by understanding the provisions of the Income Tax Act. By strategically utilising deductions under Section 80C, NPS benefits, HRA exemptions, and the standard deduction, you can significantly reduce your tax liability. Remember to plan your investments and keep all necessary documentation in order. It is advisable to consult with a tax advisor or refer to official government resources for the most current and personalised guidance relevant to your specific financial situation.
