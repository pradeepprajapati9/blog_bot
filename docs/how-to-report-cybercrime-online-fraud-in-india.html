How to Report Cybercrime & Online Fraud in India

03 Sep 2026

In today's digital world, online fraud and cybercrime are unfortunately becoming more common. From phishing scams to online transaction fraud, knowing how to react and report these incidents promptly is crucial for your safety and for helping authorities tackle these crimes. This guide provides a clear, practical, step-by-step process for Indian citizens to report cybercrime effectively, helping you understand your immediate actions, the official reporting channels, and what to expect next. Taking quick action can often limit your losses and aid investigations.

Immediate Steps After Falling Victim to Fraud

If you suspect you've been a victim of cybercrime or online fraud, your immediate actions are critical. First, do not panic. Panic can lead to mistakes. Immediately secure your accounts by changing passwords for any compromised accounts (email, banking, social media). If money has been debited, contact your bank or financial institution's fraud department immediately to report the unauthorized transaction and request a block or reversal. Many banks have dedicated 24/7 fraud helplines. Collect all possible evidence, such as screenshots of messages, transaction details, email headers, and any contact numbers or IDs used by the fraudsters. The quicker you act, the higher the chance of recovering funds or preventing further damage.

Reporting Online via the Cybercrime Portal

The primary and most efficient way to report cybercrime in India is through the official government portal: cybercrime.gov.in. This portal is managed by the Ministry of Home Affairs. To file a complaint, visit the website and select 'Report Other Cybercrime' or 'Report Women/Child Related Crime' as applicable. You will need to provide details like your contact information, the incident's date and time, a detailed description of what happened, and upload any evidence you have collected (screenshots, bank statements). After submission, you will receive an acknowledgement number, which is crucial for tracking the status of your complaint. Ensure you fill out all mandatory fields accurately for a smoother process.

Using the National Cybercrime Helpline Numbers

For immediate reporting, especially concerning financial fraud, you can also use the national helpline numbers. The primary helpline is 1930. This number connects you directly to the National Cybercrime Helpline, where you can report financial fraud in real-time. For older cases or other types of cybercrime, the number 155260 was previously used and is still operational in some regions, though 1930 is the primary contact now. When you call, be prepared to provide details about the incident, including the amount of money lost, the transaction ID, the bank involved, and the fraudster's contact details if available. This phone reporting often initiates a quick response to block fraudulent transactions.

Filing a Complaint at Your Local Police Station

While online reporting is generally preferred for cybercrime, you can also file a First Information Report (FIR) at your local police station, especially if you face difficulties with the online portal or if the crime has a physical component. Visit the nearest police station under whose jurisdiction the crime occurred or where you reside. Carry all the evidence you have gathered, including the cybercrime portal acknowledgement number if you've already filed online. Clearly explain the incident to the police officer. They will register an FIR, providing you with a copy. It's advisable to emphasize the urgency, particularly if financial losses are involved, to ensure prompt action.

Gathering and Preserving Crucial Evidence

The success of any cybercrime investigation heavily relies on the evidence you provide. Always preserve all forms of communication with the fraudster, including WhatsApp chats, SMS, emails, and call recordings. Take screenshots of suspicious websites, payment pages, or social media profiles. Note down transaction IDs, bank account numbers, UPI IDs, and dates and times of all financial movements. If an email was involved, try to preserve the original email with its full headers, as this contains technical information vital for tracing. Do not delete any data related to the incident, as even seemingly minor details can be important clues for the authorities.

What Happens After You Report a Cybercrime?

Once you report a cybercrime online, you will receive an acknowledgement number. You can use this number to track the status of your complaint on the cybercrime.gov.in portal. The reported incident is then routed to the relevant state cybercrime cell or police station for investigation. For financial fraud reported via 1930, banks and financial intermediaries are alerted immediately to try and freeze the siphoned funds. The investigation process can take time, involving forensic analysis and coordination with banks and service providers. You might be contacted by the investigating officer for further details or clarification, so be prepared to cooperate fully throughout the process.

Conclusion

Reporting cybercrime is not just about personal recovery; it's a vital step in combating online fraud and ensuring a safer digital environment for everyone in India. By acting quickly, gathering all possible evidence, and utilizing the official reporting channels – whether the cybercrime portal, the helpline 1930, or your local police station – you empower the authorities to take effective action. Stay vigilant, be aware of common scams, and remember that your prompt reporting makes a significant difference.
