How to Check Land Records Online in India: Your Guide

14 Oct 2026

Understanding who owns a piece of land, its history, and any associated liabilities is crucial, whether you're buying, selling, or simply need to verify information. Traditionally, accessing land records involved multiple visits to government offices, often a time-consuming process. However, thanks to digitalization efforts by various state governments under initiatives like the Digital India Land Records Modernization Programme (DILRMP), checking land records online in India is now much simpler. This guide will walk you through the general process, help you understand key terms, and ensure you have the necessary information to access these vital documents from the comfort of your home. It's an essential step for property owners and prospective buyers alike to ensure transparency and avoid future disputes.

Why Check Land Records Online?

Accessing land records online offers numerous benefits, primarily enhancing transparency and convenience. For prospective property buyers, verifying land records is a critical part of due diligence. It helps confirm the seller's ownership, identifies any existing encumbrances like loans or legal disputes, and verifies the property's area and type. For current landowners, it provides an easy way to monitor their property details, access documents for loans, or settle disputes. The online platforms reduce the need for physical visits to revenue offices, saving time and effort. It also minimizes the scope for fraudulent transactions by making verified information readily available to the public, fostering a more secure property market environment in India.

Key Documents & Information You'll Need

To efficiently search for land records online, you'll need specific details. While requirements can vary slightly by state, generally you should have at hand: the name of the district, sub-division (tehsil/taluk), village or block where the property is located. More specific identifiers include the Survey Number, Khasra Number, Khatauni Number, or Jamabandi Number. Sometimes, you might also be able to search using the owner's name, though this method might be less precise. Having these details ready beforehand will significantly streamline your search on the respective state government's land records portal. Ensure you have the exact spellings and numbers to avoid errors and quickly retrieve the correct information.

General Steps to Access Land Records Online

Accessing land records online typically follows a similar pattern across various state portals, though interfaces may differ. First, you need to visit the official land records portal for your specific state (e.g., Bhulekh Odisha, MahaBhulekh Maharashtra, AnyRoR Gujarat). You can find these by searching "land records [Your State Name]" on Google. Once on the portal, look for options like "View Land Records," "ROR," "Khasra/Khatauni," or "Jamabandi." You'll then be prompted to select your District, Tehsil/Taluk, and Village. Next, you'll need to enter one of the identifying numbers like Survey Number, Khasra Number, Khatauni Number, or search by owner's name. After entering the details, the system will display the relevant land records. Most portals allow you to view, download, or print these documents. Always ensure you are on the official government website to protect your data and access accurate information.

Understanding Common Land Record Terms

Navigating land records requires familiarity with specific terminology. Here are some key terms often encountered: * **Khasra Number:** This is a unique plot number assigned to a specific parcel of land in villages. It's like a survey number for rural areas and helps identify individual fields. * **Khatauni Number:** This is an account number provided to a set of co-owners who share a parcel of land. It lists the details of the land owned by a family or a group, specifying the share of each owner. * **Jamabandi:** This refers to the Record of Rights (ROR) for a specific village. It's a comprehensive document that contains information about ownership, cultivation, and other rights related to land. * **Record of Rights (ROR):** A crucial document providing details about the land's owner, type of land, its area, and any cultivation details. It also mentions any mutations (changes in ownership) that have occurred. Understanding these terms is vital for interpreting the land documents correctly.

How to Get an Encumbrance Certificate Online

An Encumbrance Certificate (EC) is a vital document that confirms a property is free from any monetary or legal liabilities, such as mortgages, loans, or ongoing disputes, for a specified period. Obtaining an EC is crucial during property transactions to ensure a clear title. Many state land registration portals or integrated service portals (like Meeseva in Andhra Pradesh/Telangana or e-Seva in Tamil Nadu) now allow you to apply for an EC online. The general process involves selecting the service, entering property details like survey number, village, and the period for which you need the certificate. You might need to pay a nominal fee online. Once the application is processed, you can often download the EC directly or receive it via post. Always verify the authenticity of the EC from the issuing authority if there are any doubts.

Conclusion

Digitalization has transformed how we access essential government services, and land records are no exception. Checking land records online in India offers an unparalleled level of convenience, transparency, and security. By following the steps outlined in this guide and understanding the key terminology, you can easily access critical information about any property. This proactive approach not only helps in making informed decisions for property transactions but also serves as a crucial tool for property management and dispute resolution. Always rely on official state government portals for accurate and up-to-date information, ensuring your property dealings are secure and hassle-free.
