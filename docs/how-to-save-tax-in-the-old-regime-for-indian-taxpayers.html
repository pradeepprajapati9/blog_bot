How to Save Tax in the Old Regime for Indian Taxpayers

11 Sep 2026

Understanding how to effectively save tax under the 'old regime' is crucial for many Indian taxpayers. While a new tax regime exists, the old regime continues to offer substantial benefits through various deductions and exemptions, provided you plan your finances strategically. This guide will walk you through the most common and impactful ways to reduce your taxable income and save money, focusing on practical steps and provisions available under the Income Tax Act. Proper planning can significantly lower your tax liability, allowing you to invest in your future while complying with tax regulations.

Maximise Section 80C Investments and Expenses

Section 80C is arguably the most popular and impactful tax-saving provision under the old regime. It allows individuals and Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs) to claim a deduction of up to Rs. 1.5 lakh from their taxable income. To make the most of this, consider investing in options like Public Provident Fund (PPF), Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS), Employees' Provident Fund (EPF), National Pension System (NPS Tier-I), and Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY). Beyond investments, certain expenses also qualify, such as life insurance premiums for yourself, your spouse, or children, principal repayment on a home loan, and children's tuition fees for full-time education. Review all your eligible payments and investments throughout the financial year to ensure you fully utilise the Rs. 1.5 lakh limit, as it's a guaranteed way to reduce your tax burden.

Utilise Section 80D for Health Insurance Premiums

Health is wealth, and saving tax while protecting your health is a dual benefit offered by Section 80D. This section allows you to deduct premiums paid for health insurance policies. You can claim up to Rs. 25,000 for yourself, your spouse, and dependent children. If you or your spouse are senior citizens, this limit increases to Rs. 50,000. Additionally, you can claim another deduction for health insurance premiums paid for your parents. This limit is Rs. 25,000 if they are below 60 years and Rs. 50,000 if they are senior citizens. A preventive health check-up expense of up to Rs. 5,000 can also be claimed within these overall limits. Paying these premiums digitally (not cash) is essential to claim the deduction effectively.

Benefit from Home Loan Interest Under Section 24(b)

If you have a home loan, the interest component offers a significant tax-saving opportunity under Section 24(b) of the Income Tax Act. For a self-occupied property, you can claim a deduction of up to Rs. 2 lakh per financial year on the interest paid on your home loan. This deduction is available from the year the construction is completed and the property is acquired. For rented-out properties, the entire interest paid on the home loan can be claimed as a deduction. However, the loss from house property that can be set off against other income in a financial year is capped at Rs. 2 lakh. Always keep detailed records of your interest certificates from your lender to accurately claim this deduction.

Claim Deductions for Charitable Donations (Section 80G)

Contributing to noble causes can also help you save on taxes. Section 80G allows taxpayers to claim deductions for donations made to certain relief funds and charitable institutions. The deduction amount can be 100% or 50% of the donated amount, either with or without any qualifying limit, depending on the recipient organisation. It is crucial to donate to institutions that are registered under Section 80G and obtain a proper receipt from them, which includes the organisation's name, address, PAN, and the amount donated. Cash donations above Rs. 2,000 are not eligible for this deduction, so ensure your contributions are made via cheque, demand draft, or digital payment methods.

Explore Other Important Deductions (80E, 80TTA, 80TTB)

Beyond the major sections, several other provisions can help reduce your taxable income. Section 80E allows you to claim a deduction on the interest paid on an education loan taken for your higher education or that of your spouse or children. There is no limit on the amount of interest that can be deducted, and it can be claimed for eight consecutive years or until the interest is fully repaid, whichever is earlier. Section 80TTA provides a deduction of up to Rs. 10,000 on interest earned from savings bank accounts for individuals and HUFs. For senior citizens, Section 80TTB offers a higher deduction of up to Rs. 50,000 on interest income from savings accounts, fixed deposits, and recurring deposits. Exploring these can add up to meaningful tax savings.

Review Your Options: Is the Old Regime Right for You?

Deciding whether the old tax regime is beneficial for you involves evaluating your potential deductions. The old regime allows you to claim numerous deductions and exemptions, which can significantly lower your taxable income if you have eligible expenses and investments. If you make substantial contributions under Section 80C, pay significant health insurance premiums (80D), have a home loan (24b), or other eligible expenses like education loan interest (80E), the old regime is likely more advantageous. In contrast, the new tax regime offers lower tax rates but allows very few deductions. It is essential to calculate your tax liability under both regimes before the end of the financial year to choose the one that results in the lowest tax for you.

Conclusion

Saving tax effectively under the old regime requires careful planning and a clear understanding of the various provisions available. By strategically utilising deductions like Section 80C, 80D, 24(b), 80G, and others, you can significantly reduce your tax burden. Remember to keep proper records of all your investments, expenses, and payments to substantiate your claims. While this guide provides a comprehensive overview of popular tax-saving avenues, always ensure your choices align with your financial goals and current tax regulations for optimal benefits.
