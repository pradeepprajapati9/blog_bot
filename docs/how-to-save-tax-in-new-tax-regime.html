How to Save Tax in New Tax Regime

05 Aug 2026

India's New Tax Regime (NTR) was introduced to simplify income tax calculations by offering lower tax rates in exchange for foregoing most traditional deductions and exemptions. This often leaves taxpayers wondering: 'How can I save tax in this regime if there are hardly any deductions?' While it's true that the NTR significantly curtails popular tax-saving avenues like Section 80C, 80D, HRA, and home loan interest benefits, it doesn't mean smart tax planning is entirely off the table. This guide will help you understand the few remaining ways to optimize your tax liability under the New Tax Regime, ensuring you make informed choices.

Decoding the New Tax Regime (NTR) Basics

The New Tax Regime is an optional, simplified tax structure for individual taxpayers, offering lower tax rates compared to the Old Tax Regime. However, this comes at the cost of giving up over 70 common exemptions and deductions. From Financial Year 2023-24 (Assessment Year 2024-25) onwards, the New Tax Regime became the default option for individuals. If you wish to continue with the Old Tax Regime and claim its deductions, you must explicitly opt for it. The biggest 'tax saving' for many under the NTR is the enhanced tax rebate under Section 87A, which effectively makes income up to Rs 7 lakh entirely tax-free for individuals. This means if your net taxable income after any allowed deductions is Rs 7 lakh or less, you pay zero tax.

The Automatic Standard Deduction Benefit

A significant relief introduced for salaried individuals and pensioners opting for the New Tax Regime from FY 2023-24 is the standard deduction of Rs 50,000. Previously, this benefit was only available under the Old Tax Regime. Now, regardless of which regime you choose, if you are a salaried employee or a pensioner, you can claim a flat Rs 50,000 deduction from your gross income. This is an automatic deduction, meaning you don't need to make specific investments or incur particular expenses to claim it. It directly reduces your taxable income, thereby lowering your overall tax liability without any extra effort on your part, making it a key component of tax saving in the NTR.

Maximizing Employer's NPS Contribution (80CCD(2))

One of the very few specific deductions still available under the New Tax Regime is for contributions made by your employer to your National Pension System (NPS) account. Under Section 80CCD(2) of the Income Tax Act, you can deduct the amount contributed by your employer to your NPS account. For private sector employees, this deduction is capped at 10% of your basic salary plus dearness allowance (DA). For Central Government employees, the limit is higher at 14%. This is a powerful tool for reducing your taxable income in the NTR, as it directly lowers your gross income before tax calculation. If your employer offers this benefit, consider opting for it to leverage this valuable tax-saving opportunity.

Understanding Exempt Allowances for Official Duties

While common exemptions like House Rent Allowance (HRA) and Leave Travel Allowance (LTA) are not available in the New Tax Regime, certain allowances provided by your employer that are genuinely for official purposes continue to remain exempt from tax. These are not 'deductions' in the traditional sense but rather incomes that are not considered taxable from the outset. Examples include: conveyance allowance granted to meet expenses incurred on official travel, travelling allowance for the cost of travel on tour or transfer, and daily allowance for ordinary daily expenses incurred on account of absence from your normal place of duty. Remember, these allowances must be used for their intended official purpose, and you might need to provide proof of expenditure if asked by your employer or the tax authorities.

Niche Deductions and Non-Taxable Incomes

Beyond the mainstream, a couple of specific, though less common, deductions are still permissible under the New Tax Regime. For instance, contributions made to the Agniveer Corpus Fund are deductible under Section 80CCH. Additionally, donations made to certain specific relief funds and institutions, as specified under Section 80G, can still qualify for a deduction, though its scope is highly limited in the NTR. It's also crucial to remember that certain types of income are inherently non-taxable regardless of the tax regime chosen. These include agricultural income, interest earned from a Public Provident Fund (PPF) account, and gratuity or leave encashment received within prescribed limits. While not 'saving' through deductions, knowing these incomes don't add to your taxable base is essential.

The Strategy of Choosing the Right Regime

Ultimately, 'saving tax' in the New Tax Regime largely boils down to whether its lower tax slabs and the Section 87A rebate (for income up to Rs 7 lakh) benefit you more than the deductions available in the Old Tax Regime. The best strategy is to perform a careful comparison. Use an online income tax calculator to compute your tax liability under both regimes. List all your potential deductions under the Old Regime (80C, 80D, HRA, home loan interest, etc.) and compare the final tax payable with the amount under the NTR, which includes the Rs 50,000 standard deduction and the Rs 7 lakh rebate. Your choice should be based on which regime results in a lower tax outflow for your specific income and investment pattern for that financial year, as you have the flexibility to choose annually.

Conclusion

While the New Tax Regime prioritizes simplicity by scaling back traditional deductions, it still offers avenues for smart tax planning. Understanding the default Rs 50,000 standard deduction, leveraging employer contributions to NPS under Section 80CCD(2), and knowing which official allowances remain exempt are key. More importantly, evaluating if the lower tax rates and the generous Section 87A rebate benefit you more than the old regime's deductions is crucial. By carefully comparing both options each year, you can make an informed decision that optimally reduces your tax liability.
