How to Use UPI in India for NRIs: A Practical Guide

09 Jul 2026

Unified Payments Interface (UPI) has transformed digital transactions in India, making payments instant, secure, and incredibly convenient. If you're an Non-Resident Indian (NRI) visiting or living abroad, you might be wondering how you can leverage this powerful tool for your financial needs back home. The good news is, NRIs can absolutely use UPI! This guide will walk you through everything you need to know to set up and use UPI effectively in India, ensuring your transactions are hassle-free whether you're supporting family, paying bills, or making purchases.

What is UPI and Why it's Great for NRIs

UPI is an instant real-time payment system developed by the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI). It allows you to link multiple bank accounts into a single mobile application, enabling you to send and receive money directly from your smartphone, 24/7. For NRIs, UPI offers unparalleled convenience. You can instantly send money to family in India, pay utility bills, shop at local stores by scanning a QR code, or even make online purchases without needing to carry cash or use physical cards. It eliminates the complexities of traditional remittances for smaller, day-to-day transactions and provides a secure, easy way to manage your finances in India, especially during visits. Its widespread acceptance across India makes it an indispensable tool for managing expenses.

Eligibility and Requirements for NRIs to Use UPI

To use UPI in India as an NRI, there are a few key requirements. Firstly, you must have an active NRE (Non-Resident External) or NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) bank account with an Indian bank that supports UPI services. This account must be fully KYC (Know Your Customer) compliant. Secondly, you need an Indian mobile number. This number does not necessarily need to be active in India, but it must be linked to your NRE or NRO bank account for verification purposes. Your bank will use this registered mobile number to send OTPs (One-Time Passwords) during registration and for authorizing transactions. Ensure your mobile number is updated with your bank and can receive SMS internationally if you are setting it up from abroad. Recently, UPI has expanded to allow international mobile numbers from certain countries to link, so check with your specific bank for the latest options.

Setting Up UPI: Linking Your Bank Account

Setting up UPI is a straightforward process. First, download a UPI-enabled payment app from your smartphone's app store. Popular options include BHIM, Google Pay, PhonePe, and Paytm. After installing, register using the Indian mobile number linked to your NRE/NRO account. The app will verify your mobile number by sending an SMS. Ensure your phone has sufficient balance or an active pack to send this verification SMS. Once verified, you will be prompted to select your bank from a list. The app will then fetch your bank account details linked to that mobile number. Choose your NRE or NRO account. Finally, you will need to set a UPI PIN for your account using your debit card details (usually the last 6 digits of your card and expiry date). This PIN is crucial for authorizing all your transactions.

Making Payments with UPI: A Step-by-Step Guide

Using UPI for payments is incredibly simple. Once your account is set up and PIN created, you can make payments in several ways. The most common methods are: sending money to a Virtual Payment Address (VPA), scanning a QR code, or using an account number and IFSC code. To send money to a VPA (like example@bankname), enter the VPA, the amount, and confirm with your UPI PIN. For QR code payments, simply open the app's scanner, scan the merchant's QR code, enter the amount, and approve with your UPI PIN. For bank account transfers, select the 'Bank Account + IFSC' option, enter the beneficiary's details, the amount, and authorize with your PIN. Most UPI apps will show the beneficiary's name for verification before you finalize the payment. Remember, there's typically a daily transaction limit of Rs. 1,00,000 for UPI.

Receiving Payments and Checking Balance

While NRIs primarily use UPI for sending money, you can also receive funds into your linked NRE/NRO account. To receive money, you simply need to share your Virtual Payment Address (VPA) with the sender. The sender can then initiate a payment to your VPA from their UPI app. Once they approve the transaction, the funds will be credited instantly to your NRE/NRO account. To check your bank account balance, open your UPI app, select the 'Check Balance' or similar option for your linked account, and enter your UPI PIN. The app will display your current balance in real-time. This feature helps you keep track of your funds effortlessly without needing to visit an ATM or log into internet banking every time.

Important Considerations and Tips for NRI UPI Users

For a smooth UPI experience as an NRI, keep these tips in mind. Always ensure you have a stable internet connection on your mobile device for transactions to go through without interruption. Protect your UPI PIN fiercely; never share it with anyone, and avoid writing it down. If you forget your PIN, you can reset it through your UPI app using your debit card details. Be mindful of the daily transaction limits, which are usually Rs. 1,00,000, but can vary slightly between banks and apps. Regularly update your UPI app to ensure you have the latest security features and improvements. In case of any transaction issues, most UPI apps offer a grievance redressal mechanism, or you can directly contact your bank's customer support for assistance.

Troubleshooting Common UPI Issues

Occasionally, you might encounter minor issues while using UPI. If a transaction fails, first check your internet connectivity. A poor signal is a common culprit. Ensure you've entered the correct UPI PIN; multiple incorrect attempts can temporarily block your account. If the recipient hasn't received funds, check your transaction history for the status. Often, failed transactions are automatically reversed within minutes to hours. If the money is debited but not credited and not reversed, raise a dispute within the app or contact your bank. For issues with mobile number registration or linking accounts, verify that your Indian mobile number is active and correctly updated in your bank records. Sometimes, simply restarting your phone or clearing the app's cache can resolve minor glitches.

Conclusion

UPI offers an incredibly convenient and secure way for Non-Resident Indians to manage their finances and make payments in India. By understanding the eligibility criteria, following the setup steps, and being mindful of security best practices, you can leverage this powerful digital payment system for seamless transactions. Whether it's supporting family, paying bills, or making purchases during your visits, UPI simplifies financial interactions with India. Embrace the ease and efficiency of UPI to stay connected to your roots, financially speaking, with just a few taps on your smartphone.
