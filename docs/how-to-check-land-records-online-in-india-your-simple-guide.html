How to Check Land Records Online in India: Your Simple Guide

14 Nov 2026

Understanding and verifying land records is a crucial step for any property owner or prospective buyer in India. These documents provide essential details about ownership, property boundaries, and legal status. Traditionally, accessing land records meant multiple visits to government offices, a process that was often time-consuming and complex. Fortunately, the Indian government's digitization efforts have made it much easier to access these vital records online. This guide will walk you through the process of checking land records online, helping you understand what to look for and how to use these digital resources effectively and accurately.

What Are Land Records & Why Are They Important?

Land records are official documents maintained by state revenue departments that contain comprehensive information about land parcels. Key documents include the Bhulekh (land records in general), Khasra (plot number), Khatauni (record of land holdings by a family), and the Record of Rights (ROR), which details ownership, cultivation, and other rights. These records are fundamental for establishing legal ownership, resolving property disputes, and ensuring transparency in land transactions. They are essential when buying or selling property, applying for a home loan, or even just confirming your property details. Accurate land records prevent fraud and provide legal backing for your ownership.

The Role of State Government Portals

Unlike a single national portal, land records in India are maintained and accessed through individual state government websites. Each state has its own designated portal, often named 'Bhulekh' followed by the state name (e.g., 'UP Bhulekh', 'Maha Bhulekh'), or specific project names like 'Bhoomi' in Karnataka. These portals are developed and managed by the respective state revenue or land records departments. To find your state's portal, simply search on Google for 'Bhulekh [Your State Name]' or 'Land Records [Your State Name]'. It is crucial to use only official government websites to ensure the authenticity and accuracy of the information you retrieve.

Step-by-Step Guide to Accessing Land Records Online

Once you are on your state's official land records portal, the process generally follows these steps. First, select your district, then your tehsil (sub-district), and finally your village from the dropdown menus. You will typically be offered several search options: by Khasra number (plot number), Khatauni number (account number), owner's name, or mutation ID. Choose the option that is most convenient for you and enter the required details accurately. After submitting the information, the portal will display the relevant land records. You can then view the details on screen or, in most cases, download a copy for your reference. Remember, these online views are usually for informational purposes, and for official use, you might need a certified copy from the local revenue office.

Key Information You'll Find on Online Land Records

When you access your land records online, you will find a wealth of information crucial for property verification. This typically includes the name of the legal owner(s), details of the land parcel such as its area in hectares or acres, and the type of land (e.g., agricultural, residential, commercial). You will also see details about any encumbrances, like existing loans or mortgages against the property, which is vital information for buyers. Furthermore, the records often show the survey number, which is a unique identification for the land, and details of any mutations (changes in ownership) that have occurred over time. Carefully reviewing all these details helps confirm the property's legal status.

Understanding Khasra, Khatauni, and ROR

Delving deeper into land record terminology, Khasra, Khatauni, and ROR are key terms you will encounter. A Khasra number is a unique plot number assigned to a specific piece of land in rural areas, used for identification and cadastral mapping. The Khatauni number represents an account of land holdings belonging to a particular family or individual in a village. It lists all the Khasra numbers owned by that entity. The Record of Rights (ROR), often referred to as 'Jamabandi' in some states, is the most comprehensive document. It contains details about the owner, cultivation details, types of soil, water rates, and any other rights or liabilities associated with the land. Understanding these terms helps you navigate and interpret the online records correctly.

Tips for Verifying Land Records & What to Watch Out For

While online land records provide immense convenience, it is essential to exercise due diligence. Always cross-verify the information displayed online with physical documents if available, especially during a property transaction. Look out for any discrepancies in names, areas, or survey numbers. Pay close attention to the 'encumbrance' section, as it reveals any legal claims or financial liabilities on the property. If you find any discrepancies or suspect fraudulent activity, it is advisable to contact the local revenue department or a legal expert immediately. For official purposes, such as applying for a loan or property registration, you may still need to obtain a certified copy of the ROR or other relevant documents from the Tahsildar's office, as the online view might not be considered legally binding proof.

Conclusion

Checking land records online has transformed property verification in India, making the process much more accessible and transparent. By understanding what these records contain and how to navigate your state's portal, you can confidently verify property details, protect your interests, and make informed decisions. Regularly reviewing your land records is a smart practice, whether you are a current owner or considering a new purchase. Always rely on official government portals for accuracy and remember that for legal validity, obtaining a certified physical copy might still be necessary.
