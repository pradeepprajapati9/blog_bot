How to Save Taxes on RSU in India

03 Sep 2026

Receiving Restricted Stock Units (RSUs) can be an exciting part of your compensation package, offering a share in your company's success. However, understanding how RSUs are taxed in India is crucial to optimize your earnings. Many employees are surprised by the tax implications at various stages. This guide breaks down RSU taxation in a simple, practical way, focusing on legitimate strategies to help you save taxes and make the most of your equity compensation.

Understanding RSU Taxation in India

In India, RSUs are generally taxed at two distinct stages: first, when they 'vest' and become your property, and second, when you 'sell' them. At the time of vesting, the fair market value (FMV) of the shares on that date is treated as a 'perquisite' or additional income and is added to your salary income. Your employer typically deducts Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) on this amount. Later, when you decide to sell these vested shares, any profit you make from the sale is subject to capital gains tax. This dual taxation structure means that effective tax planning for RSUs requires understanding both these events and applying suitable strategies at each stage.

Taxation at RSU Vesting: The Perquisite Income

When your RSUs vest, meaning the ownership officially transfers to you, the value of these shares is considered a 'perquisite' in your hands. This value is calculated based on the Fair Market Value (FMV) of the shares on the vesting date. This perquisite amount is added to your total income under the head 'Salaries' for that financial year. Consequently, it becomes taxable at your applicable income tax slab rates. Your employer is responsible for deducting TDS on this perquisite component, along with your regular salary. This will reflect in your Form 16, increasing your taxable salary income. Since this is treated as salary, standard deductions under Section 80C, 80D, and others can be used against your overall taxable income to reduce this component.

Capital Gains Tax on RSU Sale

Once your RSUs have vested and you own the shares, selling them triggers capital gains tax. The 'cost of acquisition' for calculating capital gains is the FMV on the vesting date, which was already taxed as a perquisite. The difference between the sale price and this FMV at vesting is your capital gain or loss. This gain is classified as either Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) or Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG), depending on your holding period from the vesting date to the sale date. For shares listed on a recognized stock exchange in India, a holding period of more than 12 months qualifies for LTCG. If sold within 12 months, it's STCG. Understanding these timelines is key to strategic selling.

Leveraging Deductions for Perquisite Income

While the RSU vesting value is treated as salary, you can effectively lower your overall tax liability by maximizing general tax deductions. Utilize Section 80C for investments up to ₹1.5 lakh in instruments like Public Provident Fund (PPF), Employee Provident Fund (EPF), Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS), life insurance premiums, and home loan principal repayments. Section 80D allows deductions for health insurance premiums. Other options include Section 80E for education loan interest and Section 80G for certain donations. By strategically investing and planning your expenses, you can reduce your net taxable income, thereby lessening the impact of the RSU perquisite income on your tax outgo for the financial year.

Strategies to Minimize Capital Gains Tax

To reduce capital gains tax when selling RSUs, consider the following. Firstly, aim to hold listed shares for more than 12 months after vesting to qualify for Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) treatment. Under Section 112A, LTCG on listed equity shares is taxed at 10% on gains exceeding ₹1 lakh in a financial year, with gains up to ₹1 lakh being exempt. In contrast, Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) from listed shares sold through a recognized stock exchange are typically taxed at a flat 15% (under Section 111A). Secondly, consider 'tax-loss harvesting' by offsetting capital gains with any capital losses from other investments. Lastly, if you have a substantial number of shares, selling them in tranches across different financial years can help utilize the ₹1 lakh LTCG exemption each year, thereby spreading out and potentially reducing your tax burden.

Importance of Timing and Financial Planning

Timing plays a crucial role in RSU tax planning. Selling vested shares just after 12 months can convert STCG into more favorable LTCG. Furthermore, planning your sales across financial years can help you utilize annual tax exemptions effectively. For instance, if you have gains just over the ₹1 lakh LTCG exemption limit in one year, selling a portion in the next financial year might allow you to claim the exemption again. Always align your RSU selling strategy with your broader financial goals, such as buying a house, children's education, or retirement. Don't let tax planning solely dictate your decisions, but integrate it wisely. Consulting a qualified financial or tax advisor can provide personalized guidance tailored to your specific situation and company RSU plan.

Essential Record Keeping for RSUs

Accurate record-keeping is paramount for smooth tax filing related to your RSUs. Ensure you maintain all vesting statements provided by your employer or the brokerage handling your RSUs. These statements typically show the vesting date and the Fair Market Value (FMV) of the shares on that date, which is crucial for determining your cost of acquisition for capital gains purposes. Also, keep records of all your sale transactions, including sale dates and prices. Cross-verify the RSU perquisite income reported in your Form 16 from your employer. Regular review of your Form 26AS will show all taxes deducted on your behalf. Meticulous records will simplify your tax filing process and serve as proof in case of any queries from the tax authorities.

Conclusion

Navigating RSU taxation in India can seem complex, but with a clear understanding of the rules and strategic planning, you can significantly optimize your tax liability. Remember that RSUs are taxed both as perquisite income at vesting and as capital gains upon sale. By maximizing your eligible deductions, strategically timing your sales, and maintaining diligent records, you can ensure you're making the most of your equity compensation. For personalized advice, it's always recommended to consult a professional tax advisor who can assess your unique financial situation.
