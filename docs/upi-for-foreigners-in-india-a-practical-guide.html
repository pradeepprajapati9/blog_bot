UPI for Foreigners in India: A Practical Guide

01 Aug 2026

If you are visiting India, you will quickly notice that Unified Payments Interface (UPI) is everywhere. From street vendors to large stores, UPI is the preferred and most common method for digital transactions, making cash seem almost optional. As a foreigner, you might wonder how you can tap into this incredibly convenient system. While traditionally UPI has required an Indian bank account and a local mobile number, the landscape is evolving. This guide will help you understand the current situation, emerging solutions, and practical ways for foreigners to make payments in India.

What is UPI and Why it's Essential in India?

UPI is an instant real-time payment system developed by the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI). It allows you to link multiple bank accounts into a single mobile application, enabling instant money transfers, bill payments, and merchant payments using a unique UPI ID or a QR code. Its ease of use, speed, and widespread acceptance have made it the backbone of India's digital economy. For residents, it's incredibly convenient, eliminating the need for cash or cards for most daily transactions. Foreigners often observe locals seamlessly scanning QR codes to pay for everything from a cup of tea to taxi fares, highlighting its indispensable role in modern Indian life and sparking their curiosity to use it too.

The Primary Hurdle: Indian Bank Account & Mobile Number

Historically, the biggest challenge for foreigners wanting to use UPI has been the fundamental requirement of an Indian bank account and an active Indian mobile number. To register for any UPI application (like Google Pay, PhonePe, or Paytm), your mobile number must be linked to an Indian bank account. This means that short-term tourists, who typically don't open local bank accounts or get local SIM cards, have found it nearly impossible to use UPI. While some foreigners might have an international roaming number, UPI apps specifically verify that the linked mobile number is an Indian one for security and regulatory purposes. This limitation has long been a point of inconvenience for international visitors accustomed to digital payments in their home countries.

The NRI Exception: If You Have an Indian Bank Account

For Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) or Overseas Citizens of India (OCIs) who maintain an Indian bank account (specifically an NRE or NRO account), using UPI is generally straightforward. If your NRE/NRO account is linked to an Indian mobile number, you can register for UPI apps just like any resident. In fact, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has expanded this to allow NRIs from select countries to link their international mobile numbers to their NRE/NRO accounts for UPI transactions. This makes it much easier for NRIs returning to India to leverage the convenience of UPI, provided their bank and UPI app support this specific international number linking. It's always best to check with your Indian bank if your specific international number is eligible.

The Game-Changer: UPI for International Travelers (Pilot Schemes)

Recognizing the need to extend UPI's convenience to international visitors, the NPCI has launched initiatives to allow foreigners to use UPI. During the G20 Summit in 2023, a pilot program was introduced allowing delegates from participating countries to experience UPI payments using their international bank accounts. Currently, some Indian banks (like ICICI Bank and IndusInd Bank) are facilitating UPI access for international travelers from select countries. This typically involves linking their international mobile numbers and foreign bank accounts to specific UPI applications or prepaid payment instruments. This is a significant step, but it is still in its early stages and might have limitations regarding eligible countries, banks, and the types of transactions. Always inquire at major international airports or reputable banks upon arrival for the latest information on these evolving services.

How the International UPI System (if available) Works

If you are eligible for an international UPI solution, the process usually involves a few steps. You might need to download a specific UPI-enabled app that supports international linkages. After installation, you would typically register using your international mobile number. The app would then guide you to link your foreign bank account or a pre-loaded wallet. Once linked and verified, you will be able to scan UPI QR codes at merchants across India and make payments directly from your international account. The currency conversion will happen automatically, often at competitive rates, similar to how international debit/credit card transactions work. Always ensure you understand any associated foreign exchange fees or transaction limits before using the service extensively.

Practical Alternatives if UPI Isn't an Option Yet

While international UPI is expanding, it might not be universally available or suitable for everyone just yet. In such cases, there are several reliable alternatives for making payments in India. Carrying some Indian Rupees (INR) cash is always advisable, especially for smaller vendors or in remote areas. Most larger establishments, hotels, and major stores accept international credit and debit cards (Visa, Mastercard, American Express). Prepaid travel cards are also a good option, allowing you to load money in your home currency and spend in INR, often with better exchange rates and lower fees than traditional bank cards. Additionally, mobile wallets like Paytm (for specific services like bill payments, though not directly linked to foreign accounts for merchant payments) or even international payment apps like PayPal (for online transactions) might offer limited utility.

Key Tips for Using Digital Payments in India

Regardless of whether you use UPI or alternative methods, here are some tips for a smooth payment experience in India. Always ensure you have a stable internet connection if relying on digital payments. Before initiating a transaction, confirm the amount with the merchant. When scanning QR codes, double-check that the merchant's name displayed on your app matches the expected payee. Keep an eye on your bank statements for any unauthorized transactions. While India is rapidly digitizing, it’s wise to always carry a small amount of cash for emergencies or very small purchases where digital payments might not be accepted. Familiarize yourself with your bank’s international transaction fees to avoid surprises.

Conclusion

UPI is undeniably transforming the way payments are made in India, offering unparalleled convenience and efficiency. While it has traditionally presented hurdles for foreigners, the efforts to open up UPI to international travelers are a welcome development. As these pilot programs mature and expand, more visitors will be able to enjoy the seamless experience of making digital payments across India. In the meantime, understanding the current options, exploring emerging services, and having reliable alternatives like credit cards and cash will ensure a smooth and enjoyable trip. Stay updated with the latest news from NPCI and major Indian banks for the most current information on UPI access for foreigners.
