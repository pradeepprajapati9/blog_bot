What is Mutual Funds vs ETF? Explained Simply

03 Aug 2026

When you start exploring investment options in India, two terms you're likely to encounter are 'Mutual Funds' and 'Exchange Traded Funds' (ETFs). Both are popular ways to invest in a diversified portfolio of securities like stocks or bonds without buying individual shares. However, they operate quite differently. Understanding these differences is crucial to choosing the right investment vehicle for your financial goals. Let's break down what each is and how they compare, helping you make an informed decision for your financial journey.

What is a Mutual Fund?

A mutual fund is a professionally managed investment scheme that pools money from multiple investors to invest in securities like stocks, bonds, money market instruments, and other assets. When you invest in a mutual fund, you buy units of the fund. The value of these units is determined by the fund's Net Asset Value (NAV), which is calculated once at the end of each trading day. Investors can buy or sell mutual fund units directly from the fund house or through distributors. There's a wide variety of mutual funds available, including equity funds, debt funds, hybrid funds, and index funds, catering to different risk appetites and investment objectives. They are ideal for investors seeking professional management and diversification with minimal active involvement.

What is an Exchange Traded Fund (ETF)?

An Exchange Traded Fund (ETF) is a type of investment fund that holds assets like stocks, commodities, or bonds, and typically tracks an underlying index, such as the Nifty 50 or Sensex. What makes an ETF unique is that it trades like a regular stock on a stock exchange throughout the day. This means you can buy and sell ETF units at prevailing market prices during trading hours, just like shares of a company. To invest in an ETF, you need a demat account and a trading account, similar to what you'd use for buying stocks. ETFs generally offer diversification and are known for their transparency, as their holdings often closely mirror a specific index.

Key Difference 1: Trading and Pricing

One of the most significant distinctions between mutual funds and ETFs lies in how they are traded and priced. Mutual funds are bought and sold directly from the fund house (or its distributors) at the end-of-day Net Asset Value (NAV). This means all buy and sell orders received during the day are processed at a single NAV, which is declared after the market closes. You don't know the exact price you'll get until the day's end. In contrast, ETFs trade on a stock exchange throughout the day. Their prices fluctuate continuously based on demand and supply, much like individual stocks. You can place buy or sell orders at any time during market hours, and the transaction happens at the current market price, giving you real-time price discovery.

Key Difference 2: Expense Ratios and Fees

Expense ratios are annual fees charged by fund houses for managing your investments, expressed as a percentage of your total investment. Generally, ETFs tend to have lower expense ratios compared to actively managed mutual funds. This is because many ETFs are passively managed, meaning they simply aim to replicate the performance of an index rather than actively picking stocks. Actively managed mutual funds, which involve fund managers constantly researching and selecting securities, typically have higher expense ratios. However, it's important to remember that when trading ETFs, you also incur brokerage charges and other transaction fees similar to stock trading, which might not be present when investing directly in mutual funds.

Key Difference 3: Liquidity and Transparency

ETFs typically offer higher intra-day liquidity. Since they trade on an exchange, you can buy or sell them at any point during market hours and exit your position quickly. The transparency of ETF holdings is also generally higher, with portfolio constituents often publicly available on a daily basis. For mutual funds, liquidity is based on their redemption cycle, and you place an order to be processed at the day's closing NAV. While most open-ended mutual funds allow daily transactions, the settlement might take a couple of days. Mutual fund portfolios are usually disclosed periodically, typically monthly or quarterly.

Who Should Choose Which? (Indian Context)

For Indian investors, the choice often comes down to investment style and goals. If you prefer a hands-off approach, want to invest regularly through Systematic Investment Plans (SIPs), and are comfortable with a single-day pricing model, mutual funds might be more suitable. They also offer a wider range of actively managed strategies. ETFs are often preferred by investors who have a demat and trading account, are comfortable with real-time trading like stocks, and are looking for lower-cost, passive exposure to a specific index or asset class. They can be good for those who want to actively manage their portfolio throughout the day or seek immediate price discovery. Both options offer diversification and are regulated investment vehicles.

Important Considerations for Your Decision

When deciding between mutual funds and ETFs, consider your investment horizon, risk tolerance, and familiarity with stock market trading. If you're a long-term investor focused on wealth creation and prefer a simple, disciplined approach, a mutual fund SIP could be ideal. If you're comfortable with a demat account, understand market dynamics, and prioritize lower expense ratios and intra-day trading flexibility, an ETF might be a better fit. Remember that both instruments are subject to market risks. Always understand the underlying assets, fund objective, and associated costs before investing. Your financial goals should always guide your investment choices, not just the instrument type.

Conclusion

Both mutual funds and ETFs are excellent investment tools that can help you build wealth over time by providing diversification and professional management. While they share similarities in their objective of pooling investor money, their operational mechanics – particularly regarding trading, pricing, and fees – are quite distinct. There isn't a universally 'better' option; the best choice depends entirely on your personal investment preferences, financial goals, and comfort level with market trading. Take the time to evaluate your own needs before making a decision, and remember to consult a financial advisor if you need personalized guidance.
